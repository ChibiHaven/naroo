Executive Summary
NaRoo is a mobile-first decision-support prototype for smallholder rice farmers in Roi Et Province. In response to the professor’s feedback, the project has been narrowed from a multi-crop, multi-province agricultural platform to one practical decision: whether planting mung bean after the major rice harvest is suitable for a farmer’s stated conditions. The MVP will not attempt to diagnose crops, prescribe exact chemical rates, predict yields or prices, or replace an agronomist.
The prototype uses a hybrid design. A small, transparent rule set—reviewed against credible agronomic guidance—determines whether a scenario is Suitable, Borderline, or Escalate. An LLM is used only to interpret free-text inputs and explain the rule-based result in simple Thai or English. Every recommendation shows the inputs used, sources, uncertainty, key risks, and a referral path for cases that cannot be supported safely.
Design continuity:  The existing 10-screen UI/UX is retained. Scope is narrowed through content and logic changes—not a redesign. Weather and price remain read-only supporting screens; automated alerts, live market prediction, SMS/voice, and a 500-farmer pilot move to the future roadmap.

1. Selected Sector / Industry
Sector: AgricultureIndustry focus: Digital agricultural extension and decision support for rainfed smallholder rice systems in Roi Et Province, northeastern Thailand.
2. Specific Problem and Evidence of Its Importance
Smallholder farmers often make rotation decisions with incomplete, fragmented, or generic information. Digital agriculture can widen access to advice, but smallholders may still face barriers related to affordability, literacy, connectivity, local relevance, and trust (Gumbi et al., 2023). The revised prototype therefore addresses a single decision where context matters: whether a rice field is suitable for a short-duration legume after the major rice season.
The decision is relevant because a rice–legume rotation can potentially diversify production and support soil nitrogen management, yet suitability depends on planting window, residual moisture or irrigation, drainage, field type, soil information, and local conditions. Thailand-specific research has examined soybean within integrated rotation systems in the Northeast, reinforcing the relevance of locally tested rotation approaches while also showing why recommendations must remain conditional rather than universal (Taiyawong et al., 2025).
Roi Et is selected to create a coherent local context and a realistic referral pathway. It falls within the area served by the Office of Agricultural Economics Region 4, and rice is a central crop in the region. This location replaces the earlier multi-province scope.
3. Target Users and Stakeholders
Primary user
A smallholder rice farmer in Roi Et who is deciding what to do with a lowland paddy field after the major rice harvest.
The farmer may have limited agronomic knowledge, uncertain soil information, intermittent connectivity, or low confidence using text-heavy digital services.
Secondary stakeholders
District and provincial agricultural extension officers, who can review rules and receive difficult cases.
Farmer groups or cooperatives, which can support assisted use and gather feedback.
Policymakers and public extension agencies interested in safe, scalable, and inclusive digital advisory delivery.

5. Novelty and Value
Hybrid safety architecture: trusted rules determine the recommendation; generative AI explains it accessibly.
Decision transparency: users can inspect the assumptions, rule-based reasons, sources, risks, and last-updated dates behind an answer.
Practical inclusion: icons, short forms, “unsure” choices, Thai-language output, and assisted use reduce barriers for farmers with lower digital or reading confidence.
Focused value: the app helps a farmer make one seasonal choice more confidently instead of promising an end-to-end agricultural platform.
6. Initial Implementation and Prototype Plan
6.1 Prototype workflow
The farmer opens NaRoo and selects Get Farm Guidance.
The farmer completes the existing three-step Farm Information form.
The system validates missing or conflicting inputs and lists explicit assumptions.
The rules engine classifies the case as Suitable, Borderline, or Escalate.
The LLM explains the result in plain Thai or English without changing the classification.
The Guidance Result screen shows reasons, planting window, major risks, sources, uncertainty, and a human-referral action when required.
The farmer may open the Weather Snapshot, Price Reference, Source Details, Assumptions, and Safety & Disclaimer screens.
6.2 Illustrative decision logic
Important:  The thresholds below are prototype logic for demonstration. They must be reviewed by an agronomist or extension officer before any real-world use.

Outcome
Illustrative conditions
System behaviour
Suitable
Roi Et lowland paddy; previous crop is rice; timing fits the verified post-rice window; water and drainage are adequate; no hard-risk flag.
Recommend considering mung bean; explain benefits, constraints, and next checks.
Borderline
One important factor is uncertain or marginal—for example soil information is unknown, water is limited, or planting timing is near the edge of the verified window.
Give conditional guidance, state uncertainty, and recommend confirmation with an extension officer.
Escalate
Location is outside scope; field conditions conflict; serious waterlogging/drought concern; chemical-rate request; pest/disease diagnosis; or insufficient inputs for safe advice.
Do not make a suitability claim. Explain the limitation and show a verified human-referral route.

8. Key Risks, Responsible-AI Considerations, and Controls
Risk
Control
Unsafe or unsupported advice
Rules-first classification; restricted prompt; source requirement; prohibited-content checks; escalation on missing evidence.
LLM hallucination
LLM receives only structured inputs, decision trace, and approved text; output checked for unsupported claims before display.
False certainty
Suitable/Borderline/Escalate labels, explicit assumptions, key risks, source dates, and “change inputs” option.
Over-reliance
Safety acknowledgement and visible reminder that local conditions and professional advice take precedence.
Literacy and language barriers
Thai-language option, icons, short sentences, limited choices, “unsure” responses, and cooperative-assisted use.
Connectivity and access
Lightweight screens and saved results; SMS/voice fallback postponed until after the MVP.
Privacy
Collect only farm-decision inputs; avoid national ID, exact home address, financial credentials, and unrelated personal data.
Stale weather or prices
Display source and timestamp; label information as snapshot/reference; do not guarantee accuracy or future values.
Unverified referral information
Use a placeholder during development and publish a contact only after verifying it with the relevant Roi Et/district office.

11. Adoption and Policy Considerations
Use trusted intermediaries—farmer groups, cooperatives, and local extension staff—to introduce the tool and support first-time use.
Offer Thai as the default language for field testing; use plain terminology, visual cues, and confirmation screens.
Design for assisted use on a shared device rather than assuming every farmer owns a smartphone or has continuous data access.
For policymakers, position NaRoo as an extension-support tool with an auditable rule base, not as a replacement for public agricultural officers.
Before any public deployment, establish content ownership, update schedules, contact verification, incident reporting, and approval of agronomic rules.
